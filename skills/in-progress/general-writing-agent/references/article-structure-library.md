# Article Structure Library

Use these as flexible patterns, not rigid formulas. The article's
purpose and evidence determine the final structure.

## A. Emotional resonance and life insight

**Purpose:** Help readers recognize and rethink an emotional or life
situation.

1.  Open with a specific, recognizable scene or tension.
2.  Name the hidden problem beneath the surface emotion.
3.  State the central insight clearly.
4.  Explain the insight through distinct layers, examples, or
    consequences.
5.  Reframe the reader's understanding without dismissing real
    constraints.
6.  Close with a grounded reflection or useful next step.

**Avoid:** emotional manipulation, diagnosing readers, universal claims
from one anecdote, and sentimental conclusions unrelated to the
argument.

## B. Personal experience and lessons

**Purpose:** Turn a real experience into transferable learning.

1.  Establish the real context and stakes.
2.  Describe the challenge, mistake, or constraint.
3.  Show the turning point with concrete details.
4.  Explain what changed in the user's thinking or behavior.
5.  Extract principles or steps.
6.  Explain where the lesson applies---and where it may not.
7.  End with a practical takeaway.

**Evidence rule:** Do not invent dialogue, dates, results, motivations,
or scenes. Ask the user or mark gaps.

## C. Argument and conceptual analysis

**Purpose:** Persuade through clear reasoning.

1.  Introduce a question, contradiction, or observable phenomenon.
2.  State a precise thesis.
3.  Define important terms and explain the mechanism.
4.  Develop two to five claims, each with reasons and support.
5.  Use examples that genuinely support the claims.
6.  Consider a counterargument, limitation, or boundary condition.
7.  Derive implications, criteria, or a conclusion.

**Avoid:** straw-man arguments, false dilemmas, unsupported causal
claims, and repeating the thesis as if repetition were proof.

## D. Practical guide and action

**Purpose:** Help the reader solve a specific problem.

1.  Define the reader's goal and current obstacle.
2.  Identify common ineffective approaches.
3.  Explain the principle behind a better approach.
4.  Give sequenced, actionable steps.
5.  Include a realistic example or demonstration.
6.  Explain likely obstacles and adaptations.
7.  End with the smallest useful action.

**Avoid:** vague advice ("坚持就好"), steps without context, and
guarantees of results.

## E. Commercial value and ethical conversion

**Purpose:** Explain a relevant offer and help readers make an informed
decision.

1.  Identify the audience need and situation.
2.  Explain the problem and its consequences accurately.
3.  Establish relevant credibility using verifiable experience or
    evidence.
4.  Explain the proposed solution and its limits.
5.  Describe confirmed product/service details.
6.  Address reasonable fit, cost, time, and outcome questions.
7.  Invite the reader to take a clear, voluntary next step.

**Avoid:** fabricated testimonials, fake scarcity, inflated promises,
hidden conditions, and fear-based pressure.

## Useful hybrid patterns

-   **A+B:** emotional recognition followed by a real personal lesson.
-   **B+C:** personal experience as the entry point to a reasoned
    argument.
-   **C+D:** conceptual explanation followed by practical steps.
-   **A+B+E:** empathy and relevant experience followed by a transparent
    offer.

## Outline quality test

For each section ask: - What new information or reasoning does it add? -
How does it advance the thesis? - What evidence supports it? - Could it
be removed without weakening the article?

If two sections perform the same job, combine or differentiate them.
